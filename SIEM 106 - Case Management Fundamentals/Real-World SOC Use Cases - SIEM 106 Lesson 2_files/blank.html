Investigating a Phishing Email

Phishing remains one of the most common ways attackers trick users. SOC teams need a consistent way to investigate and escalate these attempts.

When a phishing email is reported by a user or detected in Falcon, analysts must act quickly to determine impact and prevent further spread. A Case centralizes artifacts, escalations, and notifications so all actions are captured.

Scenario

It’s 2:30 PM on a Friday afternoon. A sales employee forwards an email to the SOC team with the subject line: Urgent: Invoice Attached.

Phishing emails are one of the most common entry points for attackers. Documenting the report in a Case, attaching artifacts, and escalating as needed ensures the SOC responds effectively and maintains SLA compliance. If a user has reported a phishing email, you must take the following action:

  • Step 1

    Create the Case

    When a phishing email is reported or detected, the first step is to create a Case. This provides a central workspace to gather artifacts, assign ownership, and start the acknowledgement SLA timer.

  • Step 2

    Assign to Analysts

    Assign the Case to Tier 1 analysts for triage. They validate the report, collect details, and determine whether it is likely malicious.

  • Step 3

    Attach Artifacts

    Attach the phishing email and supporting artifacts, such as headers, links, or screenshots. This ensures a complete audit trail and prepares the Case for escalation if needed.

  • Step 4

    Document Findings and Continue Case Work

    If analysis confirms the phishing attempt is malicious, document the findings and continue working the Case. Any notifications to teams such as Incident Response, IT, or Legal occur automatically based on the SLA timers and notification paths defined in the Case template.

Notifications help maintain SLA compliance by alerting the right teams when time thresholds are reached. Escalation or reassignment occurs manually, for example, when a Tier 2 analyst takes ownership. Closing a Case too soon may cause missed evidence or compliance gaps, but SLAs are only breached when defined timelines are exceeded.

Investigating phishing activity requires both speed and accuracy. By creating a Case, assigning it, attaching artifacts, and escalating when appropriate, analysts maintain SLA compliance and ensure the organization responds effectively to this common attack vector.
