Roles in Case Management

Successful Case management depends on clearly defined roles.

Effective Case management requires both strategic oversight and hands-on investigation. In Falcon, these responsibilities are shared between two key roles:

  1. 1

    The Case Manager sets structure, tools, and oversight.

  2. 2

    The Analyst executes investigations within that framework.

Let’s meet each role in more detail.

A Case Manager acts as the orchestrator of the Case process. They focus on structure and oversight, not individual investigations.

Responsibilities include:

  • Design Templates: Build and maintain Case templates and workflows.

  • Notification Groups: Create and manage escalation groups.

  • Automation & SLAs: Define automation rules and service-level agreements.

  • Reporting: Monitor Case metrics and generate team performance reports.

  • Escalations: Step in when Cases exceed SLAs or need special handling.

A Security Analyst is the primary case handler. Their focus is technical investigation and resolution.

Responsibilities include:

  • Create cases from templates or scratch.

  • Investigate detections through triage and deep analysis.

  • Assign relevant artifacts for case tracking and analysis.

  • Respond with containment and remediation actions.

  • Document case notes and update statuses.

  • Notify stakeholders using notification groups.

  • Adapt workflows for unique incidents.

Scenario

Meet Lisa and Shawn, who work together at a financial services company. Lisa is the Case Manager responsible for setting up processes, while Shawn is the Analyst handling day-to-day investigations. Let’s see how their roles connect during a phishing attempt.

  1. 1

    Lisa, the Case Manager, provides a Phishing Case template with defined workflows, escalation rules, and notification groups.

  2. 2

    Shawn, the Analyst, applies the template to the phishing detection, then investigates and contains affected accounts.

  3. 3

    Lisa monitors Case metrics and ensures the process aligns with SLAs, while Shawn keeps the Case updated with artifacts and comments.

If a Case isn’t acknowledged in time, the SLA alerts the notification group defined in the SLA so it can be reassigned before deadlines are missed.

Other Roles

Additionally, below are additional roles that are involved in the Case Management process.

Role

Key Responsibilities

Case Manager

• Create and maintain Case templates
• Define workflows, SLAs, and escalation paths
• Monitor Case quality, metrics, and reporting
• Oversee automation escalations triggered by notification groups, SLAs, and pre-defined escalation pathways.

Security Analyst

• Create Cases from detections or templates
• Perform triage, investigation, containment, and remediation
• Document findings and update Case status
• Notify stakeholders and escalate Cases when needed

Incident Response Lead / Team

• Provide guidance for high-severity or complex Cases
• Coordinate cross-team response (such as IT, Legal)
• Approve containment or remediation strategies
• Capture lessons learned

IT / Operations Teams

• Execute remediation tasks (such as isolate endpoints, reset accounts)
• Provide technical support for containment and recovery
• Confirm remediation completion in the Case

Legal / Compliance Teams

• Review Cases for compliance or regulatory requirements
• Ensure breach reporting obligations are met
• Validate Case documentation for legal standards

SOC Manager / Team Lead

• Oversee SOC performance metrics (MTTA, MTTR, SLA compliance)
• Align Case handling with organizational goals
• Identify process gaps and drive improvements
• Allocate resources for escalations or critical Cases

The Case Manager sets up the framework while the SOC Analyst applies it in a real investigation. Together, they resolve the Case efficiently, with the right teams automatically informed through escalation pathways defined in the SLAs.
