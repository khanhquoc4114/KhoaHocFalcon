Why Workflow Separation Matters

When everyone gets every alert, no one gets what they need.

When operational and security alerts are mixed, teams can waste valuable time and miss critical signals. Before diving into the specific risks, it’s important to understand that each alert type has its own audience, priority, and handling process. Clearly distinguishing between these ensures that alerts reach the right people and are acted upon appropriately.

In this lesson, you'll understand the importance of separating operational and security workflows to reduce confusion, improve efficiency, and ensure the right teams respond to the right signals.

The Risks of Mixed Workflows

If operational and security workflows are combined, several problems can occur:

  • •

    SOCs spend time investigating benign system issues – This diverts attention away from real threats.

  • •

    IT teams miss critical performance issues buried in threat alerts – Essential maintenance can be delayed or overlooked.

  • •

    Confusion over alert ownership delays resolution – No one knows who should respond, leading to slower remediation.

Best Practices

To avoid the risks above and keep workflows clear and efficient, follow these guidelines:

An alert for "Service Restart Failure" is routed to the SOC and investigated as a threat. Two hours later, it’s discovered it was part of a scheduled maintenance task.

Result: A misclassification led to wasted time and delayed real threat investigation.
